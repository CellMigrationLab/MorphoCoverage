# Adversarial audit: does MorphoCoverage answer the microscopist's questions?

**Date:** 2026-09-25. **Scope:** the three moments of use (report, plan,
online) of `CellMigrationLab/MorphoCoverage` at commit `ec80e53`, audited for
the person who has to decide how much to image. **Method:** every question
was followed through the code that computes it, the sentence that prints it
and the decision it supports, then executed on the committed idr0028 subset
and on constructed acquisitions whose truth is known. Nothing here was taken
on trust from documentation or tests. The reproduction scripts are described
under each finding; every number in this document was produced by them in
this session.

The short answer to the central question:

- **Trust it** for the well mean of a scalar readout, judged on held-back
  fields, with all its caveats printed (Layer 2 reliability, calibration and
  adequacy). On idr0028 the package correctly reports the null result that
  the JCB cell-number rule and a fixed 4–8-field rule are enough for that
  readout, and it correctly reports, on a constructed spatial gradient, that
  every simple rule stops too early in a raster order.
- **Ignore it, as shipped,** on three answers that the released idr0028
  report gave wrongly or without the qualifier that changes the decision: the
  variance curve and protocol line (a reservoir ceiling presented as a field
  requirement), the "98% of the variance is between samples" line (3 wells,
  interval includes zero), and the object-unit JCB verdict printed without
  its independence assumption. These are fixed in wording in this branch.
- **It still cannot tell you** whether you imaged enough for rare or
  minority phenotypes, whether a technical batch shift is hiding in pooled
  data, whether your features detect the phenotype you care about when the
  declared control is weak, or how many fields to plan for a rare state.
  The report is silent, or nearly so, on all four.

## 2. Findings ranked by harm to the experiment

Every finding names the scenario, the code, the exact setup, what the
microscopist sees, what they would conclude, why that is wrong or unclear,
the expected behaviour, and whether it was confirmed by execution. "Fixed"
means fixed in this branch, in wording only; no estimator, threshold,
objective or public API changed.

### Critical

**F1. The object-unit JCB verdict is printed as "enough" without its assumption, and it is enough after one field.** *Layer 1.* Scenario: any sample with spatial structure. Code: `sufficiency._enough_answer`, `strategies/scalar_precision.py`. Setup: `sufficiency_report(..., strategies=[ScalarPrecisionStrategy("feature_a", 0.05, "object", "upper_95")])` on 3 homogeneous FOVs (E1), and `stopping_calibration` on the `neighbour_correlation` scenario (E9). Seen: "scalar_precision_object_upper95 (averaging_cv <= 0.05): yes, met by the 3 acquired FOVs; first met after 1 FOVs". On the gradient, the same rule stops after 2 fields and is premature in 100% of 5 recorded-order and 68% of 25 random-order replays for the mean within 5%. Conclusion drawn: one or two fields are enough. Why wrong: the published rule answers "how many cells to average in a spatially uniform sample"; the sentence does not say that cells are treated as independent draws, and `sufficiency_report` is self-assessment, so nothing in that report contradicts it. The calculation is faithful to Zweifach 2024 (the worked example is reproduced in `tests/test_scalar_precision.py`); the harm is presentation and the README's example, which pairs the rule only with a fixed-FOV rule. Reproduction: `python e1_few_fovs.py`, `python e9_gradient_orders.py` (scripts in the audit scratchpad; equivalent code is in `tests/test_microscopist_audit.py`). Expected: the verdict carries its assumption, and the report recommends reading it beside the FOV-unit variant and the calibration. **Fixed (wording):** the sentence now ends "(treats observations as independent draws: assumes a spatially uniform sample)" whenever the estimator reports `assumes_spatial_uniformity`. Proposed, needs approval: make `sufficiency_report` warn when only object-unit rules are given, and change the README example to show both units. Confirmed.

### High

**F2. The protocol layer turned a reservoir ceiling into "every well needs more than 18 fields".** *Layer 2.* Scenario: idr0028, "the variance of YAP/TAZ ratio within 10%". Code: `retrospective/protocol.py` (`protocol_summary`, difficulty). Setup: the shipped `report_steps_idr0028.py`. Seen (shipped report, section 4): "18 fields: enough for 0 of 12 samples ... Needs more fields than could be judged: B6, 1A (more than 18); I23, 1A (more than 18); ... (all 12 wells)". Conclusion drawn: the variance needs more than 18 fields per well. Why wrong: `reliability_ceiling_idr0028.csv` shows that all 18 stream fields together met the criterion in only 20–70% of hold-outs (median 30–40%); the 10 held-back fields cannot pin the variance to 10%, so no field count can reach the 90% threshold, and the answer is "this criterion cannot be judged with this reservoir", which `docs/preliminary_findings.md` entry 4 already states but the protocol summary did not. Reproduction: `python e4_ceiling.py`; `tests/test_microscopist_audit.py::test_protocol_names_samples_capped_by_their_reference_instead_of_needing_more_fields`. Expected: samples whose ceiling is below the threshold are named apart and not counted as needing more fields. **Fixed:** `protocol_reliability` now carries each sample's ceiling (`difficulty.ceiling`, `ceiling_below_threshold`) and the summary says "The held-back fields could not judge this for 12 of 12 samples, even with every available field, so these are not counted as needing more fields". The regenerated shipped report shows it. Confirmed.

**F3. The shipped reliability curves omit the ceiling sentence, so a flat variance curve reads as "more fields do not help".** *Layer 2.* Scenario: idr0028 variance within 10%. Code: `data/crick_idr/report_steps_idr0028.py` rebuilt `ReliabilityResult(curve, DataFrame(), GROUPS, {"labels": ...})` from the saved CSV without the ceiling table; `reliability_summary` printed nothing when the ceiling was absent. Seen: "2 fields: 27% ... 18 fields: 30% (±48% across hold-outs) / Judged on 10 or more held-back fields ..." and no ceiling line, although `docs/preliminary_findings.md` says "The report states the ceiling for this reason". Conclusion drawn: 18 fields are no better than 2. Why wrong: the median variance error falls from 0.26 at 2 fields to 0.11 at 18 (entry 4); the curve is capped at the ceiling. Reproduction: `python e4_ceiling.py` (with ceiling: sentence present; without: absent). **Fixed:** the script loads `reliability_ceiling_idr0028.csv` and `n_splits`; a result rebuilt without a ceiling now prints "How often all 18 available fields together were enough was not computed ... may be capped by them rather than by the fields". Confirmed.

**F4. "98% of the variance of the mean is between samples" from three wells, repeated in the sampling profile without a caveat.** *Layer 2 (and the Layer 1 profile).* Scenario: idr0028 effort, and a null simulation. Code: `retrospective/effort.py::effort_summary`, `retrospective/report.py::_profile`. Setup: shipped `effort_projection(..., hierarchy=SamplingHierarchy("position_id", "well_id"), strata=["condition"])`; null: 3 samples × 10 FOVs × 30 cells with zero between-sample variance, 20 seeds (E7). Seen: "Most of the uncertainty of the mean is between samples (98% ...)" for LATS1 and 99% for YAP, then "cannot tell from these data (only 3 samples)"; section 3 repeats "98% of the variance of the mean is between samples [condition=LATS1]" with no qualifier. `effort_idr0028.csv`: the LATS1 well component is 3.6e-4 with interval [-6e-6, 5.2e-4]; YAP [-5e-6, 4.9e-4]; both include zero. In the null simulation 4 of 20 runs attributed 62–70% to the samples. Conclusion drawn: image more wells and fewer fields. Why wrong: with 3 samples (2 degrees of freedom) the share is not established, and the same data are consistent with no between-sample variance. The "cannot tell" sentence concerns the allocation comparison only; the share sentence contradicts it in spirit, and the profile drops it. Reproduction: `python e7_effort_null.py`; `tests/test_microscopist_audit.py::test_a_variance_share_from_few_samples_is_marked_as_not_established`. **Fixed (wording):** the share now ends "; not established: only 3 samples, its interval includes zero" (in both the summary and the profile) whenever there are fewer than 5 independent units or the component's bootstrap interval includes zero. Confirmed.

**F5. A rare state seen in exactly two neighbouring fields is invisible to the next-FOV novelty and to the "new state" inspection reason, and nothing in the report says it exists.** *Layer 1.* Scenario: `rare_clustered` (10 rare cells in each of `img_030` and `img_031`, 40 FOVs, 5% FOV incidence). Code: `strategies/support_novelty.py` (support from one other FOV, `min_other_fovs=1`), `sufficiency._next_fov_answer`, `retrospective/inspection.py::_novel`. Seen: "Chance: 0% ... 0 of 40 FOVs showed something the others did not"; the inspection lists the two fields only under the stage-neighbour reason ("4.0 SD above its 4 nearest fields"), which needs stage coordinates. With `min_other_fovs=2` both are listed with 7 and 10 cells. Conclusion drawn: there is no minority state in this well. Why unsupported: the definition of new is "not supported by another field"; two adjacent fields of one patch support each other, which the module docstring warns about, but no printed sentence does, and the report has no line for "states seen in few fields". The rubric's question "Did I image enough for rare/minority phenotypes?" is not answered anywhere in the report: the next-FOV block is population evidence. Reproduction: `python e17_recurrent_rare.py`. Expected: the report states states seen in fewer than *r* fields (a `min_fov_occurrences` view of what was seen), and the novelty block says that recurrence in *m* fields counts as seen. Not fixed: this needs a decision on what "few" means (AGENTS.md section 15). Confirmed.

**F6. A weakly detected positive control does not qualify the adequacy answers.** *Layer 1.* Scenario: reference and positive wells differing by 0.8 SD in every feature (E5). Code: `retrospective/report.py::microscopist_report` (the "Read with care" note is added only when `controls is None or status == "not assessed"`). Seen: section 1 "recognized as different in 41% of 200 checks; 5 planted cells recognized in 1%", then section 2 "0 of 20 FOVs showed something new ... unseen states in at most 14% of FOVs". Conclusion drawn: sampling is adequate. Why wrong: with these features a field of the declared phenotype is missed 59% of the time and five such cells almost always; the 14% bound is on states this definition detects, which the phenotype of interest is not. Expected: when the detection rate is below a declared level the note says so next to every adequacy answer. Not fixed: the level is a scientific choice. Confirmed.

**F7. The online decision has no reason line, and a population-objective novelty stop can be read as rare-state adequacy.** *Layer 3.* Scenario: `SupportNoveltyStrategy(..., quantity="lofo_novel_fov_upper")` with tolerance 0.25 on any quiet sample. Code: `results.py::StrategyResult` (no text), `online.py`. Seen: `Decision.STOP` with `lofo_novel_fov_upper=0.24` after 11 fields, whatever the data, because with nothing new the bound is `1 - 0.05 ** (1 / k)`; `docs/preliminary_findings.md` entry 3 shows that at that stop a state in 5% of fields had not been seen in 46–49% of acquisitions that contained it. `component_decisions` correctly reports only `population`, and a balanced objective returns `unsupported` for the rare component (E13), so the package does not claim rare adequacy; but an operator sees a stop and a number. Expected: a one-line reason on every result ("stop: nominal 95% bound on a new state in the next field is 24%, below 25%; 0 of 11 fields showed anything new; this says nothing about states rarer than about 3 / 11") and, in `docs/online.md`, that a novelty-bound stop is a next-field statement, not rare coverage. Not fixed (new API surface). Confirmed by reading and by the tracked frequency control.

## 3. Cross-layer consistency

**Report ↔ planning.** Where Layer 1 says a well was "enough" at `k` fields, how often do random `k`-field acquisitions preserve the outcome in Layer 2? For idr0028 and the well mean: the object-unit JCB rule says enough at 2 fields in every well; `sampling_reliability` gives 98–100% at 2 fields for the mean within 5% and 83–100% (median 90%) within 2.5%; `stopping_calibration` gives 0–2% premature at 5% and 9% (max 18%, 40% recorded) at 2.5%. Consistent for the tolerance the rule was designed for; the report does not say which tolerance its "yes" corresponds to (a 5% averaging CV is a standard error, not a 5% error bound), and only Layer 2 makes the distinction visible. For the variance the layers disagreed only because of the ceiling omission (F2, F3), now removed. The two quantities answer different questions (a rule's own criterion versus a hidden-field outcome), the report says so in its module docstring, and after this branch the sufficiency sentence carries the rule's assumption; the microscopist report still does not print the two side by side.

**Planning ↔ online.** When Layer 3 stops at `k`, does Layer 2 show that stop is safe? Layer 3 and incremental replay agree exactly (0 mismatches in 60 results), and replay is what `stopping_calibration` uses, so the calibration is a valid check of the live rule. For the JCB rule on idr0028: safe at 5%, 9–18% premature at 2.5%. On a raster over a gradient: 100% premature for every simple rule. For a novelty-bound stop: the tracked frequency control shows it is a next-field statement that misses 5%-incidence states in half the acquisitions. Nothing online tells the operator which of these regimes they are in (F7).

**Representation ↔ adequacy.** The package does make strong adequacy claims when its declared control is poorly detected (F6): 41% detection beside "unseen states in at most 14%". The one existing safeguard (the "Read with care" note) only fires when no control is declared. The sampling profile prints both numbers on adjacent lines, which is the only place the reader can connect them.

**Simple ↔ complex.** For the well mean of the YAP/TAZ ratio in idr0028, the cell-number rule, a fixed 4–8-field rule and the FOV-unit variant of the JCB rule perform the same, and the report says so (a null result correctly permitted and stated in `docs/preliminary_findings.md` entry 5). The richer estimators (`FOVBootstrapStrategy`, `SupportNoveltyStrategy`, `IncidenceCoverageStrategy`) have not been benchmarked on a question where the simple rules fail (the variance, a quantile, a rare state), so no regime where they add value has been shown; the constructed gradient shows that a dispersed order helps more than any rule does. No layer silently overrides another: `sufficiency_report` is self-assessment by design, the retrospective analyses score against hidden fields, and each says which it is. The place where one overrode the other was the shipped report script (F2, F3), which lost the ceiling between Step 1 and Steps 4 and 10.

## 4. Missing advice and data requirements

**Layer 1.** Rare/minority adequacy: needs an incidence view of what was seen (states in fewer than `r` fields) and, for a claim about frequency, independent phenotype labels or a validated novelty definition with a measured false-alarm rate. Representation: needs a declared positive control at the effect size that matters, and a reference; without them every "nothing new" answer is conditional and should say so more loudly. Technical shift: needs a between-repeat or between-batch diagnostic, and stage coordinates for a within-well drift check. Empty fields: the acquisition record (a FOV table from the microscope), not a table rebuilt from cells. Missing values: a stated missing-data policy per feature.

**Layer 2.** Fields per sample: deeper acquisitions (60 or more fields per sample) so that a reservoir of 20 or more fields can judge the variance and tails, and so that `k` up to 40 can be read; the current 28-field wells stop the curve at 18. Rare-state planning: the observed FOV incidence, a declared `RareTarget`, and a reservoir design that does not remove the few positive fields (or a leave-positive-fields-in scheme with its own contract). Across samples: at least five independent units (plates or repeats declared as such, not inferred), samples nested in repeats without confounding, and a held-out dataset the thresholds were not tuned on (none is registered). More fields or more samples: the same five or more units, the acquisition cost per field and per sample for an equal-budget comparison (only equal total fields is implemented), and a repeat level that is not confounded with the sample. Spatial acquisition: stage coordinates, per-field and per-move times, and a criterion hard enough to separate orders. Sampling-frame bias: every candidate position from an overview (not implemented).

**Layer 3.** A reason line per decision; a rare-state strategy with live labels for any objective other than population; the time available before the next acquisition, to compare with the measured 5 ms to 6 s decision latency; a representation control run before the session so that the novelty definition's false-alarm rate is known for these features.

## 9. Third pass: a held-out dataset and the last two findings

- **Held-out dataset.** `data/crick_idr/datasets.py` registers idr0028 as development and idr0139 (10 wells × 4 fields, never used to choose a setting) as held out; `check_tuning` refuses a run that claims to be tuned on it. `heldout_idr0139.py` runs the development conclusions on it (`docs/preliminary_findings.md`, entry 7): "2 fields are enough for the mean within 5%" held in 12 of 12 development wells and holds in 5 of 10 held-out wells; the JCB rule's 1-field stop is premature at 5% in a median 33% of acquisitions (up to 77%). The count does not transfer; the regime (cell-number guidance is enough where fields vary little) does. The dataset is too shallow to set a number, and the report says so through its ceiling lines.
- **F11, rare-state planning line.** With `few_fovs`, a state seen in `s` of `k` FOVs now gets "Planning for such a state: seen in 5% of FOVs (95% interval 0.6%–17%); to see it at least once with 95% probability, image about 59 independent representative FOVs at the observed incidence, up to 489 at the low end of the interval. Fields that cluster the state need more." The binomial incidence model behind it was checked against the tracked planted-state control: the share of acquisitions that had seen the planted state after `k` fields matches `1 - (1 - p)^k` within 4 percentage points at every `p` (5, 10, 20%) and `k` (5, 10, 20) (`tests/test_crick_idr.py`). The fixed-reservoir reliability curve is not used for this line, so its thinning does not enter.

The assumption is stated in the sentence.
