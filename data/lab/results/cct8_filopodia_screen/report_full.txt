What this acquisition was asked to preserve
  - Population summary: the mean of Filopodia number over all cells, within 5% and 2.5% of its value
    on held-back fields.
  - Phenotypic fingerprint: not declared (no distribution criterion in fingerprint), so the answers
    below speak of the mean, not of the distribution of phenotypes.
  - Rare phenotype: not declared (no RareTarget in rare), so no answer below says whether a rare
    state was sampled enough.
  - Representation: readout Filopodia number; 1 declared feature column for unusual cells (against
    the control cells, their repeats pooled) and new phenotypes; definition of new: support_novelty.
  - Report-only rule: with this definition of new, 'Did I image enough?' also judges whether the
    chance that one more field shows something new is at most 5% (its upper bound; novelty_target).
    It is not one of the stopping rules (rules): the live decision does not run it, and optimise and
    benchmark do not calibrate it. A state that fewer than 3 other fields show is counted as seen in
    few fields (few_fovs).
  - Stopping rules: JCB rule, per cell, 5% [scalar_precision_object_upper95 (feature=Filopodia
    number, target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=object, sd_estimate=upper_95)] and JCB rule, per field,
    5% [scalar_precision_fov_upper95 (feature=Filopodia number, target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=fov,
    sd_estimate=upper_95)] (the default, rules=None: the JCB rule per cell and per field at 5%);
    assessed in the report, calibrated by optimise, run live. The JCB rules stop on the averaging
    CV: a CV of 5% is one standard error of 5% of the mean, not a bound of ±5% on its error; whether
    the mean is within 5% and 2.5% is judged on held-back fields (report, optimise), never by the
    rules.
  - Acquisition order: no acquisition_order_col or stage_cols, so nothing is checked and every stop
    treats the fields as a random sample of the well's positions; a raster over a spatial trend can
    stop early. Image fields in a random or dispersed order.
  - Live decision: the population objective (population summary); a rule decides only the targets it
    has evidence about, and a target no rule decides stays unsupported rather than assumed adequate.

  Assumptions:
    - A state below the not-yet-seen bound can remain: rarer states can remain.
    - A state seen in few fields (fields counted as new are not among them) is not new to the next
      field but is not established either: it counts as recurrent, so look at those fields before
      calling the sample settled.
    - Fields that cluster a state need more fields than the planning numbers.
    - JCB rule, per cell, 5% treats cells as independent draws: it assumes a spatially uniform
      sample.
    - The next-field bound is approximate: the leave-one-field-out outcomes share their reference
      fields.
    - The novelty bounds treat fields as independent, representative draws: neighbouring fields
      acquired in a raster over patchy states make them too low.
    - Every novelty bound holds at a number of fields fixed in advance, not at the first field where
      it passes: stopping there is premature more often than 5% of the time.
    - A state carried by fewer than 3 similar cells per field is never counted, however many fields
      hold it.
