What this acquisition was asked to preserve
  - Population summary: the mean of Filopodia number over all cells, within 5% and 2.5% of its value
    on held-back fields.
  - Phenotypic fingerprint: not declared (no distribution criterion in fingerprint), so the answers
    below speak of the mean, not of the distribution of phenotypes.
  - Rare phenotype: not declared (no RareTarget in rare), so no answer below says whether a rare
    state was sampled enough.
  - Representation: readout Filopodia number; 1 declared feature column; unusual cells are not
    assessed, as each field holds one cell; new phenotypes (new: at least 3 similar cells that no
    other field shows) are not assessed, as each field holds one cell.
  - Stopping rules: mean-precision rule, per cell, 5% [scalar_precision_object_upper95
    (feature=Filopodia number, target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=object, sd_estimate=upper_95)] and
    mean-precision rule, per field, 5% [scalar_precision_fov_upper95 (feature=Filopodia number,
    target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=fov, sd_estimate=upper_95)] (the default, rules=None: the
    mean-precision rule per cell and per field at 5%); assessed in the report, calibrated by
    optimise, run live. The mean-precision rules stop when the standard error of the mean is 5% of
    the mean (its averaging CV), not on a bound of ±5% on its error; whether the mean is within 5%
    and 2.5% is judged on held-back fields by optimise, never by the rules or this report.
  - Acquisition order: no acquisition_order_col or stage_cols, so nothing is checked and every stop
    treats the fields as a random sample of the well's positions; a raster over a spatial trend can
    stop early. Image fields in a random or dispersed order.
  - Live decision: a stop needs every declared target (population summary); a rule decides only the
    targets it has evidence about, and a target no rule decides stays unsupported rather than
    assumed adequate.

  Assumptions:
    - Across biological repeats, the paired t-test on the repeat means (as in SuperPlots) treats
      each biological repeat as one replicate and assumes the differences (raw) or the log ratios to
      the control (normalised) are normally distributed and independent between biological repeats.
      It keeps false positives at 5% when the effect varies between biological repeats, which the
      cell-level test does not; the cost is power: with 3 biological repeats and 30 cells a well, d
      1.5 is found in 98, 90 and 63% of experiments and d 1.0 in 85, 67 and 39% as biological
      repeats differ by 0, 0.25 and 0.5 cell SD (data/simulations/test_choice.py). p uncorrected, q
      corrected (Benjamini-Hochberg within each sub-experiment: the conditions sharing their repeats
      and control).
    - Reagents of one target that go the same way in every repeat speak against an off-target effect
      of one of them; this is described, not tested, since they share the control.
    - Cell-level test (Cohen's d, randomization over the cells): it assumes the cells are
      independent draws, so it asks whether these wells differ, not whether the effect repeats, and
      it responds with one or two repeats. Limitation: when the difference varies between repeats it
      gives p ≤ 0.05 without an effect in about 2·P(Z > 1.96/√(1 + n·s²/2)) of experiments (n cells
      per well, each repeat weighted by its share when they differ; s the SD between repeats in
      observation SDs; data/simulations/test_choice.py and unbalanced.py), the 'with no effect'
      column; more cells per well raise it, and a stricter cutoff does not remove it (22% at p ≤
      0.01 with 30 per well and s = 0.5).
    - Normalised: each value divided by the mean of its own repeat's control, which removes a scale
      both wells of a repeat share (staining, imaging) and pairs every condition with its own
      repeat's control. It assumes a positive feature on a ratio scale (not computed when a control
      mean is 0 or below); it does not remove the part of the effect that varies between repeats, so
      the cell-level chance still applies.
    - Balanced: one permutation test against the control of the condition's own repeats, d the
      median over 100 draws taking the same number of cells from each repeat, p from the condition
      and control labels shuffled within each repeat, the same statistic on the same draws for each
      shuffle. In data/simulations/unbalanced.py, with one repeat holding 2 to 8 times the cells of
      the others, the test as provided gives 22-46% false positives where equal repeats give 16% (s
      = 0.25), and the balanced test 15-19%; with the control pooled from repeats the condition was
      not imaged in, the raw test as provided gives 46% even when the repeats agree, the balanced
      test about 5%. Balancing removes the weight of the repeats holding more, not the independence
      assumption.
    - Within a repeat the conditions are described by their effect sizes; they are tested across
      repeats (the first table).
    - The test and the effect sizes compare means: a difference confined to a subpopulation of cells
      or to the shape of the distribution is not tested (see the unusual cells and the rare label).
    - Repeats needed: for a paired t-test at p ≤ 0.05/m, m the conditions of the sub-experiment
      (what Benjamini-Hochberg asks at most), on the values that test compares: each repeat's change
      (its mean over its control's, a fold change; the difference of the means for a feature with a
      value that is not positive; the table gives their geometric mean, as the verdict does), its
