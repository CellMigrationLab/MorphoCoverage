  Assumptions: Across biological repeats, the paired t-test on the repeat means (as in SuperPlots)
    assumes normally distributed differences; Bonferroni-corrected over the conditions. Reagents of
    one target that go the same way in every repeat speak against an off-target effect of one of
    them; this is described, not tested, since they share the control. The cell-level test treats
    the cells as independent draws: it asks whether these wells differ, and it responds with one or
    two repeats. When the difference varies between repeats, it gives p ≤ 0.05 without an effect in
    about 2·P(Z > 1.96/√(1 + n·s²/2)) of experiments (n cells per well, s the SD between repeats in
    observation SDs, as simulated in data/simulations/test_choice.py). Whether the effect repeats is
    what the paired test across repeats asks. Within a repeat the conditions are described by their
    effect sizes; they are tested across repeats (the first table). The test and the effect sizes
    compare means: a difference confined to a subpopulation of cells or to the shape of the
    distribution is not tested (see the unusual cells and the rare label). The fields a side per
    repeat are the most any repeat needs, projected from its interval of d (narrowing as
    1/sqrt(fields)); limited by: whether the repeats' d differ more than their sampling error, which
    more fields cannot reduce. Both are estimated from the repeats here, so the plan is a guide.
