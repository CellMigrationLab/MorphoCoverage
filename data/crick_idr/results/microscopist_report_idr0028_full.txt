      field but is not established either: it counts as recurrent, so look at those fields before

      spatially uniform sample.

      fields, covered the true averaging error about 85-95% of the time with uneven field sizes and
      93-99% with Poisson sizes.

      can stop early.
    - The next-field bound is approximate: the leave-one-field-out outcomes share their reference
      fields.
    - The novelty bounds treat fields as independent, representative draws: neighbouring fields
      acquired in a raster over patchy states make them too low.
    - Every novelty bound holds at a number of fields fixed in advance, not at the first field where
      it passes: stopping there is premature more often than 5% of the time.
    - A state carried by fewer than 3 similar nuclei per field is never counted, however many fields
      hold it.
    - Each acquisition is one hold-out and one order of the same fields, not an independent
      experiment.

➖ 9. Do the conditions differ, and how confidently?
  Feature YAPTAZ_Ratio, each condition against the control (MOCK), within each biological repeat. The
    fields of a condition's analysis groups are pooled for this comparison. Nuclei are compared as
    Plot&Stats does and, since a microscope acquires them through fields, with the field as the unit
    and, where each side has at least two, with the technical repeat (technical_repeat) as the unit.
  Reading, condition by condition, against MOCK. Significant across repeats: the paired t-test on the
    biological repeat means (as SuperPlots; values normalised to each repeat's control) gives q ≤ 0.05
    after Benjamini-Hochberg within the condition's sub-experiment: this is the result to report. Same
    direction, not significant: on one side of the control in every repeat without reaching q ≤ 0.05,
    a consistent trend and not evidence of no effect (with 3 repeats only large, consistent effects
    reach it), said from 6 repeats: with fewer, every repeat goes the same way by chance too often (1
    experiment in 4 with 3 repeats). Beside it, the nucleus-level screen (as Plot&Stats, every nucleus
    an independent draw) is exploratory: it finds candidates, not effects that repeat:
    - LATS1 — not tested across repeats: higher in 2 of 2 biological repeats (Cohen's d per repeat,
      mean +1.00, mean 6.9% higher than the control); the paired test needs 3 biological repeats;
      nucleus-level screen (exploratory) q 0.001 (balanced, normalised; with no real effect it gives p
      ≤ 0.05 in about 83% of experiments like this one).
    - YAP — not tested across repeats: lower in 2 of 2 biological repeats (Cohen's d per repeat, mean
      -1.35, mean 8.8% lower than the control); the paired test needs 3 biological repeats;
      nucleus-level screen (exploratory) q 0.001 (balanced, normalised; with no real effect it gives p
      ≤ 0.05 in about 79% of experiments like this one).
    - ARAP2 — not tested across repeats: higher in 2 of 2 biological repeats (Cohen's d per repeat,
      mean +0.32, mean 2.2% higher than the control); the paired test needs 3 biological repeats;
      nucleus-level screen (exploratory) q 0.001 (balanced, normalised; with no real effect it gives p

  Assumptions:
    - LATS1, YAP and ARAP2, with 2 biological repeats holding both sides, are described by direction
      and effect size; the paired test across repeats needs 3.
    - Nucleus-level test (Cohen's d, randomization over the nuclei): it assumes the nuclei are
      independent draws, so it asks whether these wells differ, not whether the effect repeats, and
      it responds with one or two repeats. Limitation: when the difference varies between repeats it
      gives p ≤ 0.05 without an effect in about 2·P(Z > 1.96/√(1 + n·s²/2)) of experiments (n nuclei
      per well, each repeat weighted by its share when they differ; s the SD between repeats in
      observation SDs; data/simulations/test_choice.py and unbalanced.py), the 'with no effect'
      column; more nuclei per well raise it, and a stricter cutoff does not remove it (22% at p ≤
      0.01 with 30 per well and s = 0.5).
    - Normalised: each value divided by the mean of its own repeat's control, which removes a scale
      both wells of a repeat share (staining, imaging) and pairs every condition with its own
      repeat's control. It assumes a positive feature on a ratio scale (not computed when a control
      mean is 0 or below); it does not remove the part of the effect that varies between repeats, so
      the nucleus-level chance still applies.
    - Balanced: one permutation test against the control of the condition's own repeats, d the
      median over 100 draws taking the same number of nuclei from each repeat, p from the condition
      and control labels shuffled within each repeat, the same statistic on the same draws for each
      shuffle. In data/simulations/unbalanced.py, with one repeat holding 2 to 8 times the cells of
      the others, the test as provided gives 22-46% false positives where equal repeats give 16% (s
      = 0.25), and the balanced test 15-19%; with the control pooled from repeats the condition was
      not imaged in, the raw test as provided gives 46% even when the repeats agree, the balanced
      test about 5%. Balancing removes the weight of the repeats holding more, not the independence
      assumption.
    - No well-level test within a repeat in all 6 contrasts: only one technical repeat (well) with
      observations on one side, no within-repeat technical replication: the fields of one well are
      not independent replicates, so there is no test.
    - The test and the effect sizes compare means: a difference confined to a subpopulation of
      nuclei or to the shape of the distribution is not tested (see the unusual nuclei and the rare
      label).
    - The draws resample these fields and nuclei, not wells or repeats: a same-sign share describes
      these fields and is not a confidence in the effect.
    - Repeats needed: for a paired t-test at p ≤ 0.05/m, m the conditions of the sub-experiment
      (what Benjamini-Hochberg asks at most), on the values that test compares: each repeat's change
      (its mean over its control's, a fold change; the difference of the means for a feature with a
      value that is not positive; the table gives their geometric mean, as the verdict does), its
      spread between the repeats and its sampling error within one. All projected from the repeats
      here, so the plan is a guide.
