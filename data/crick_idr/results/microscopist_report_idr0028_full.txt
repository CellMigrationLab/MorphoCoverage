What this acquisition was asked to preserve
  - Population summary: the mean of YAPTAZ_Ratio over all nuclei, within 5% and 2.5% of its value on
    held-back fields.
  - Phenotypic fingerprint: not declared (no distribution criterion in fingerprint), so the answers
    below speak of the mean, not of the distribution of phenotypes.
  - Rare phenotype: not declared (no RareTarget in rare), so no answer below says whether a rare
    state was sampled enough.
  - Representation: readout YAPTAZ_Ratio; 30 declared feature columns for unusual nuclei (against
    the control, every value relative to the control of its repeat; exploratory) and new phenotypes
    (new: at least 3 similar nuclei that no other field shows).
  - Report-only rule: with this definition of new, 'Did I image enough?' also judges whether the
    chance that one more field shows something new is at most 5% (its upper bound; novelty_target).
    It is not one of the stopping rules (rules): the live decision does not run it, and optimise and
    benchmark do not calibrate it. A state that fewer than 3 other fields show is counted as seen in
    few fields (few_fovs).
  - Positive control: fields of YAP judged against the control MOCK after 10 reference fields,
    paired within each repeat when the FOV table has a repeat column (pair_cols default), across the
    whole table otherwise. If fewer than 90% of its checks are recognized, a provisional threshold,
    every adequacy answer carries a warning.
  - Stopping rules: mean-precision rule, per nucleus, 5% [scalar_precision_object_upper95
    (feature=YAPTAZ_Ratio, target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=object, sd_estimate=upper_95)] and
    mean-precision rule, per field, 5% [scalar_precision_fov_upper95 (feature=YAPTAZ_Ratio,
    target_cv=0.05, resampling_unit=fov, sd_estimate=upper_95)] (the default, rules=None: the
    mean-precision rule per nucleus and per field at 5%); assessed in the report, calibrated by
    optimise, run live. The mean-precision rules stop when the standard error of the mean is 5% of
    the mean (its averaging CV), not on a bound of ±5% on its error; whether the mean is within 5%
    and 2.5% is judged on held-back fields by optimise, never by the rules or this report.
  - Acquisition order: every stop, live and in the report, is printed with a check of the fields
    seen so far for a trend of the field means of YAPTAZ_Ratio with 'acquisition_order' (Spearman)
    and alike means in neighbouring fields by stage_x_um and stage_y_um (Moran's I, permutation
    p-value), with the stage extent covered beside a rare-phenotype stop. A flagged trend is a
    caution, never a veto (it does not turn stop into continue), and finding none does not show the
    fields to be a random sample of the well's positions. Image fields in a random or dispersed
    order.
  - Live decision: a stop needs every declared target (population summary); a rule decides only the
    targets it has evidence about, and a target no rule decides stays unsupported rather than
    assumed adequate.

  Assumptions:
    - Shown: the 95% interval of d, from resampling the technical repeats and their fields as
      acquired, empty fields included, excludes 0. With fewer than 5 fields a side, not assessable.
      The fields a side it would take are projected from the part of the interval within wells
      (narrowing as 1/sqrt(fields)); the part between wells does not shrink with more fields, and
      when it alone is as wide as the difference, more fields will not resolve it: more wells or
      biological repeats. Beyond 4 times the fields acquired a difference is called too small to
      show.
    - A state below the not-yet-seen bound can remain: rarer states can remain.
    - A state seen in few fields (fields counted as new are not among them) is not new to the next
      field but is not established either: it counts as recurrent, so look at those fields before
      calling the sample settled.
    - Fields that cluster a state need more fields than the planning numbers.
    - Mean-precision rule, per nucleus, 5% treats nuclei as independent draws: it assumes a
      spatially uniform sample.
    - Mean-precision rule, per field, 5%: in simulations its upper bound, a bootstrap of whole
      fields, covered the true averaging error about 85-95% of the time with uneven field sizes and
      93-99% with Poisson sizes.
    - Order check: no trend with acquisition order or spatial autocorrelation flagged at the
      Bonferroni threshold of 0.025 (Spearman p = 0.053 to 0.93; Moran's I permutation p = 0.037 to
      0.84) over 28 fields; every stop still treats the fields as a random sample of the well's
      positions; a raster over a spatial trend can stop early.
    - The next-field bound is approximate: the leave-one-field-out outcomes share their reference
      fields.
    - The novelty bounds treat fields as independent, representative draws: neighbouring fields
      acquired in a raster over patchy states make them too low.
    - Every novelty bound holds at a number of fields fixed in advance, not at the first field where
      it passes: stopping there is premature more often than 5% of the time.
    - A state carried by fewer than 3 similar nuclei per field is never counted, however many fields
      hold it.

  Assumptions:
    - With 2 biological repeats holding both sides, LATS1, YAP and ARAP2 are described by direction
      and effect size; the paired test across repeats needs 3.
    - Nucleus-level test (Cohen's d, randomization over the nuclei): it assumes the nuclei are
      independent draws, so it asks whether these wells differ, not whether the effect repeats, and
      it responds with one or two repeats. Limitation: when the difference varies between repeats it
      gives p ≤ 0.05 without an effect in about 2·P(Z > 1.96/√(1 + n·s²/2)) of experiments (n nuclei
      per well, each repeat weighted by its share when they differ; s the SD between repeats in
      observation SDs; data/simulations/test_choice.py and unbalanced.py), the 'with no effect'
      column; more nuclei per well raise it, and a stricter cutoff does not remove it (22% at p ≤
      0.01 with 30 per well and s = 0.5).
    - Normalised: each value divided by the mean of its own repeat's control, which removes a scale
      both wells of a repeat share (staining, imaging) and pairs every condition with its own
      repeat's control. It assumes a positive feature on a ratio scale (not computed when a control
      mean is 0 or below); it does not remove the part of the effect that varies between repeats, so
      the nucleus-level chance still applies.
    - Balanced: one permutation test against the control of the condition's own repeats, d the
      median over 100 draws taking the same number of nuclei from each repeat, p from the condition
      and control labels shuffled within each repeat, the same statistic on the same draws for each
      shuffle. In data/simulations/unbalanced.py, with one repeat holding 2 to 8 times the cells of
      the others, the test as provided gives 22-46% false positives where equal repeats give 16% (s
      = 0.25), and the balanced test 15-19%; with the control pooled from repeats the condition was
      not imaged in, the raw test as provided gives 46% even when the repeats agree, the balanced
      test about 5%. Balancing removes the weight of the repeats holding more, not the independence
      assumption.
    - No well-level test within a repeat in all 6 contrasts: only one technical repeat (well) with
      observations on one side, no within-repeat technical replication: the fields of one well are
      not independent replicates, so there is no test.
    - The test and the effect sizes compare means: a difference confined to a subpopulation of
      nuclei or to the shape of the distribution is not tested (see the unusual nuclei).
    - The draws resample these fields and nuclei, not wells or repeats: a same-sign share describes
      these fields and is not a confidence in the effect.
    - Repeats needed: for a paired t-test at p ≤ 0.05/m, m the conditions of the sub-experiment
      (what Benjamini-Hochberg asks at most), on the values that test compares: each repeat's change

      value that is not positive; the table gives their geometric mean, as the verdict does), its
      spread between the repeats and its sampling error within one. All projected from the repeats
      here, so the plan is a guide.
    - The fields a side per repeat are the most any repeat needs, projected from the part of its
      interval of d within wells (narrowing as 1/sqrt of the number); the part between wells stays,

      draw and so finds differences that may not repeat; only the paired t-test on the repeat means
