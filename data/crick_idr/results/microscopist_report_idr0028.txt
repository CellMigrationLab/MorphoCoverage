⚠️ 3. What is limiting my sampling?
  - Fields: a mean from whole fields varies 2.1 times as much as one from as many random nuclei
    (median; 1.0-3.4 over 12 groups); 16% of fields differ more than random nuclei would (5%
    expected).
  - Unusual nuclei (farther from the MOCK reference than all but 1% of its nuclei; 0-3% of each
    group): clustered in a few fields (p ≤ 0.05) in 4 of the 11 groups where seen (LATS1 · 1A · I23,
    MOCK · 2A · K16, YAP · 2A · N23 and 1 more); there, seeing them takes about 1.9 times the fields
    a count of nuclei predicts.
  - Largest share of the variance of the mean: between fields within a sample in MOCK (54%); between
    samples in LATS1, YAP and ARAP2 (79-99%).
  - Neighbouring fields: in 5 of 12 groups the point estimate suggested that closer fields are more
    alike, but shuffling their positions within the group gave as large a distance in a median of
    38% of shuffles, so no spatial redundancy is shown there.
  - New states come from 4 fields (0 to 11.8 of the 336 fields judged expected by chance).
  - How well the declared control is seen: question 1.
  ⚠️ The largest share of the variance of the mean is not established: only 3 samples, its interval
     includes zero.
  ⚠️ Refits with other reference samples agree on only 67-86% of the unusual nuclei in 7 of 12
     groups (MOCK · 1A · B6, LATS1 · 1A · I23, LATS1 · 1A · J23 and 4 more; below 90%): treat those
     labels as unstable.
  Assumptions: The field counts assume fields from independent technical repeats: a state held by
    few technical repeats is clustered in their fields, so seeing it needs fields from more
    technical repeats than the count says (no two-level plan is made).
